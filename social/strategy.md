# Eastpoint social strategy (instructions for the clip agent)

Keep this file in step with the "Eastpoint Social Media Strategy: The Front Door" doc.
The clip agent reads it every week.

## Who we are
Eastpoint Church Durham is a gospel-centered church that meets Sundays at 10am at Oak
Grove Elementary School (3810 Wake Forest Hwy, Durham, NC). Mission: to point a hurting
world to hope in Jesus. Vision: to bring hope to the Triangle and the nations as we
proclaim and embody the good news of Jesus. Bible translation: NIV.

Core values: Engagement (we celebrate the image of God in every person), Growth (we
prioritize gospel community), Action (we empower mercy-filled lives), Witness (we tell
stories of real hope), Faith (we expect God to change lives).

## Why social
Social media is our front door. Most people meet Eastpoint on a phone weeks before they
visit. We are reaching neighbors in East Durham and the Triangle who are curious,
hurting, or far from church; young families near Oak Grove; Christians new to Durham;
and our own people, who share clips with friends.

## Non-negotiables
- Jesus is the hero of every clip. A clever clip that never gets to the gospel does not ship.
- Honest about pain, confident in hope. Name hurt without being bleak or preachy.
- No clickbait, outrage, or politics-as-bait. Hooks create curiosity; they never
  misrepresent what was said.
- Show scripture references in NIV form, e.g. "Luke 18:1-8 NIV".
- Never name a congregant or guest; skip clips that do.

## Voice
Warm, plain-spoken, a little playful, never churchy. Like a friend who loves Jesus and
loves Durham. Local words where true: Durham, East Durham, the Triangle, Oak Grove.
- Say "You don't have to have it together to come Sunday", not "All are welcome to join
  us for worship".
- End on what Jesus does, not on what you should do better.
- Curious hooks, never clickbait: "The judge in this story is the opposite of God", not
  "You WON'T BELIEVE what Jesus said".

## Caption rules (post copy)
Editorial and unhurried, in the spirit of Bridgetown Church's Instagram: the teaching
carries the post, the copy frames it.
- First line: the clip's question or central statement, in plain words, under 90
  characters ("Who is your rabbi?", "Prayer isn't a technique. It's a relationship.").
- Then 1 to 2 short paragraphs (2 to 3 sentences each) that open up the idea for someone
  who wasn't in the room. Warm, reflective, concrete. No hype, no exclamation marks.
- Then the source line: From "<Series>: <Message title>" (use the sermon title given).
- Last line: eastpointdurham.com. When a clip works best as an invitation, end instead
  with "Gather with us / Sundays at 10am, Oak Grove Elementary".
- No hashtag walls and no emoji. At most two local tags on Instagram (#Durham #EastDurham),
  on their own last line; none on Facebook.
- Facebook: same copy; may add one warm sentence of invitation.
- YouTube Shorts title: under 70 characters, a searchable question or phrase, ends with
  #shorts; description = first paragraph + "Full message: <link>".

## Clip selection rubric
Pick 2 to 3 moments (quality over quantity) that each stand alone for someone who has never been to church.

A good clip:
- Makes one idea land in 30 to 75 seconds (hard max 88).
- Opens strong: the first 3 seconds are a question, a tension, a surprising line, or a
  vivid story beat. Never starts with "So", "And", "As I was saying", or a reference back.
- Makes sense with zero context: no "this passage", "like we said earlier", or
  unexplained names.
- Lands on Jesus, grace, or hope, not on a to-do list.
- Starts and ends on a full sentence, never mid-thought.

Score each candidate 1 to 5 on: hook, standalone, gospel clarity, emotional truth,
shareability. Only keep clips that average 3.5 or better.

Skip: announcements, offering, welcome, inside jokes, anything naming a congregant,
laughter with no payoff, the scripture reading on its own, prayer at the end, and
moments that depend on slides or props people can't see.

Make each clip a different kind (gospel-clear, practical or comforting, story, or an
answer to a question skeptics ask), so the week's options give the team real choices.

## Using the reels
Each week's reels are options, not a schedule: the team posts whichever fit, whenever
suits. Every reel comes with its own ready-to-use copy for Instagram, Facebook and
YouTube Shorts. Keep clips under 90 seconds (Facebook Reels limit).
